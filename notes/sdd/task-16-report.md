## 3. Deviations from the brief and decisions (please rule on 3.1)

### 3.1 Decision 1, paragraph grouping: implemented with a cap
The literal rule ("join while gap ≤ 2 × the page's median line gap") cannot work on the corpus. On most pages the median gap is the paragraph gap, not the line gap. I measured the white space between consecutive lines outside tables on every text page:
- **33 of the corpus's 52 text pages have a median of 9.0 pt**, which is the gap between paragraphs. That covers every page of supply-01, -04, -05, -06, nda-01 and mixed-01, and pages 1–4 and 24 of supply-10.
- Only supply-10's pages 5–23 have a median of 3.0 pt.
- On a page of short paragraphs, such as definitions, notice blocks or signatures, most gaps are paragraph gaps. The signature page has nothing but 9 pt gaps.

With the literal rule, the limit on those pages is 18 pt, so everything merges:
- supply-01 gives **31** paragraph and heading blocks against **107** in its Word twin.
- Its page 1 body collapses into 3 paragraph blocks, one of them 175 words long.
- supply-10 gives 301 blocks against 370.

**What I implemented:** a line joins the one above it when four conditions hold:
- both are headings or both are not;
- their sizes are within 0.5 pt;
- the gap is **≤ 2 × the median gap** (the median of positive gaps; at least 3 are needed for a median);
- the gap is **< 0.6 × the font size**. That is your 6 pt for 10 pt text, and it is also the fallback when a page has too few lines for a median.

Your rule becomes the limit wherever it is the tighter of the two, so tightly set documents are still split by it (`test_paragraphs_set_close_together`). The result matches the Word twins exactly: 107 of 107 for supply-01 and 370 of 370 for supply-10, also checked for supply-04 and -05. I chose a font-relative limit over a fixed 6 pt so that larger or smaller type behaves the same way. All of this lives in `_same_block` and two constants, so a different ruling is a small change.

### 3.2 Other choices
- **`Block` gained a fifth field `size: float = 0.0`** (font size in points, 0 for a table). Decision 5's Heading 1/2 split needs it, and the brief's dataclass has no size. The four-field constructor `Block(kind, text, rows, page)` still works.
- **`ScannedPdfError` when no page has any text**, not only when every page is image-only. This covers the brief's case and also a scan with a blank page, and a blank or outlines-only PDF, which would otherwise become an empty Word file. `test_a_pdf_without_any_text_is_refused` pins it.
- **Running lines: the 60 % is counted over pages with text** (an image-only page cannot hold a running line), **and needs at least 2 pages**. Otherwise a one-page PDF would move its top and bottom lines to the front. The kept occurrence follows the heading rule; the corpus's 8.5 pt lines are paragraphs.
- **Line tolerance `min(0.5 × median char size, 5 pt)`** instead of pdfplumber's 3 pt. The corpus's footnote marks sit 3.42 pt above their line. At 3 pt such a mark becomes its own line and is read before the line it belongs to, so it could land inside a value that wraps. The 5 pt cap came from self-review (section 5).
- **XML-illegal characters are dropped.** A probe showed that a PDF with an embedded TrueType font gives pdfplumber `"Alpha\x00Beta"`, and python-docx then raises `ValueError` (a crash, not a `MaskError`).

**IMPORTANT 1 (R37): stamped scans.**

**IMPORTANT 2 (R38): hidden layers.**
- `_hidden_layers(document.doc)` reads `catalog["OCProperties"]["D"]`. A layer counts as off when it is in its /OFF array, and also under `/BaseState /OFF` (every layer off except those in /ON), the other way a default configuration turns layers off.
- Layer names are decoded with pdfminer's `decode_text`.
- When any layer is off, every object tagged `"OC"` is dropped before anything is read.
- New field: `PdfReport.hidden_layers: list[str] = field(default_factory=list)`, so the three-field constructor still works.
- The approximation is documented in the module docstring, as pdfplumber's code behaves. Its `begin_tag` sets `cur_tag` to the innermost tag and `end_tag` resets it to None. While a layer is off:
  - text in layers that are **on** is dropped too;
  - hidden text inside a marked-content sequence nested in the layer, or after one, is still read. A tagged PDF does this, with /P or /Span inside /OC.

  Note that the second point is the opposite direction from the ruling's wording ("may drop a little visible text").
- The new fixture `hand_pdf(path, contents, layers=...)` writes the reviewer's handmade shape: the layer /oc1 "Internal notes", /F1 Helvetica, and /F2, a CID font without ToUnicode. It includes a FontDescriptor, so pdfminer logs no FontBBox warning.

## OPEN 2 (R49): hidden layers by a marked-content stack

**Which layers are off.** `_layers_off(document)` returns the layers that are off by default, as object number → name: the /D config's /OFF array, plus every /OCGs entry not in /ON when /BaseState is /OFF. If there is no /OCProperties, nothing is hidden. If /OCProperties is not a dict, has no /D, or /OFF, /ON or /OCGs is not an array of references, the PDF is refused.

**The second pass.** When some layer is off, `_hidden_chars(page, off)` runs a second pdfminer pass over the page. It uses two classes:
- **`_LayerDevice(PDFPageAggregator)`** keeps the stack. `begin_tag` pushes the visibility of the sequence (an /OC sequence, or a shown entry for any other tag) and `end_tag` pops it. `begin_figure` pushes the visibility of the form being drawn, and `end_figure` pops it and closes any sequences the form left open. `render_char` notes (text, y0, hidden, layers involved) for every character.
- **`_LayerInterpreter(PDFPageInterpreter)`** feeds the device.
  - `do_BDC` resolves an /OC sequence's property name in **the resources of the stream being drawn**: the page, or a form's own resources, since pdfminer gives forms a sub-interpreter.
  - `do_Do` hands the device the XObject's own /OC before its figure begins.

  **(choice)** R49 describes doing the resolution in the device. I split it this way because pdfminer gives `begin_tag` only the unresolved name, and `begin_figure` only the XObject's name. Inside a form, only the interpreter knows the resources in force. The stack itself is in the device's `begin_tag`/`end_tag`/`begin_figure`/`end_figure`, as ruled.

**Visibility rules.** An OCG is hidden when it is in the off set. An OCMD is evaluated by its /OCGs and /P policy (AnyOn by default, AllOn, AnyOff, AllOff; an empty membership hides nothing), or by its /VE expression (And, Or, Not) when it has one.

**Matching to pdfplumber's characters (choice).** pdfplumber lists characters in drawing order: pdfminer adds a form's `LTFigure` at the form's end, and `iter_layout_objects` recurses in place. So the device's notes are **paired one by one** with `page.chars`, and each pair must agree on text and y0 (within 0.01 pt). Any mismatch, or any error in the second pass, refuses the PDF.

I chose this over the suggested 0.5 pt coordinate match. Pairing is exact even when hidden text is drawn exactly over visible text, as a redaction or redline layer can be, where coordinates would drop both.

**What happens next.** Hidden characters are dropped with the copies and the rotated characters. The R38 "drop every OC tag" approximation is gone, and text in a shown layer is kept.

**Refusal.** An /OC that cannot be resolved (an unknown property name, something that is neither an OCG nor an OCMD, an unknown policy, a malformed /VE) refuses the PDF. So does an unreadable configuration. The error is `UnsupportedFileError(LAYERS_MESSAGE)`, with R49's exact text.
